## 2. What the numbers say

1. **Sanity anchor holds.** Eigen bo20 at 0 % is *exactly* the public repo's own
   `trainval.py --test` on this box (eth 0.365/0.582, hotel 0.147/0.220) and matches the paper's
   ET-STGCNN row. The data path, checkpoints and metric are right.
2. **Best-of-1 from ET-STGCNN is not a point predictor.** The model emits 20 anchor-refined
   samples with no likelihood; their final points are spread 2–3 m on ETH/UCY. The sample the
   flown adapter would have taken — `preds[0]`, i.e. raw sample 0 — scores **1.39 / 2.88**,
   worse than plain constant velocity (0.52 / 1.14) and near hold-last on some scenes. Medoid
   and mean are also worse than CV. The only rule that reaches CV level is "the sample nearest
   the CV continuation" (0.541 / 1.161), which is what the shim now puts at index 0. **Its
   bo20 (0.23 / 0.38) is an oracle** — a single-goal follower cannot use it without a
   selector. *The paper's "learned branch contributes multimodality" claim is true of the set,
   not of what a bridge can fly.*
3. **With linear interpolation the learned model is insensitive to missing steps** (0.540 →
   0.543 ADE from 0 % to 50 %): a 3.2 s pedestrian window is nearly straight, so the imputed
   window is nearly the true one. **With forward-fill — the repo's own HELD-frame behaviour —
   it degrades steeply** (0.541 → 1.435). The "assumes a complete history" sensitivity in the
   paper's Sec. Trajectory Prediction is therefore **a property of the imputation, not of the
   predictor**: if the perception node keeps republishing the last position on a missed range
   sample, the learned branch is the arm that suffers.
4. **The flown CTRV cannot be evaluated on this protocol as-is.** `_predict_future_xz`
   estimates heading from two 6-sample baselines, so it returns nothing from fewer than 8
   points — **100 % fallback at every non-zero missing rate** — and it starts the forecast from
   an EMA(0.30) of the positions, which at 0.4 s spacing lags ~0.9 s, which is why it scores
   1.17 / 1.66 even at 0 % (eth 1.74 vs CV 1.00). These are sample-count constants tuned for an
   8–30 Hz detector, where the history is 60 points and the EMA lag is ~0.3 s; they are not
   bugs in flight. But it means the "CTRV only" row cannot be the flown code verbatim.
5. **CTRV-fit ≈ CV, and the turn-rate state earns nothing.** A gap-native CTRV with the repo's
   own clips and its yaw-rate consistency guard sits at CV level (0.62 vs 0.52) and is flat
   across missing rates. Without the guard the LS turn rate costs 0.10–0.25 m ADE: pedestrians
   do not hold a turn for 4.8 s. On ETH/UCY at this horizon, CTRV = CV.
6. **Fusion.** At the code's α = 0.2 the fused predictor improves on CTRV-fit by 0.03 m at 0 %
   and loses to it from 30 % missing on with ffill. The best constant α moves from 0.8 (0 %)
   to 0.2 (30 %) to 0 (50 %) — the schedule the paper asks for exists in the data. The
   requested schedule **α = 0.2 · n_observed / 8** (0.2, 0.175, 0.15, 0.10) is too gentle to
   matter (≤ 0.07 m at 50 %); a schedule that reaches 0 by 50 % missing is what the sweep
   supports. With interp imputation, α = 0.2 is a small, consistent win at every rate
   (0.559 / 1.192 at 50 % vs CTRV-fit 0.574 / 1.220).

**For the paper.** Table II can be filled from `table.md` with: CTRV only = CTRV-fit (state
that it is the flown motion model re-fitted on observed stamps); Learned only = Eigen
best-of-1 nearest-CV *and* bo20 marked as an oracle; Fused = CTRV-fit ⊕ Eigen at α = 0.2,
plus the sweep. All of it is public-checkpoint, offline, `\bench{}`-class at best — none of
it flew.

## 4. Exact protocol

- **Data.** Public repo `utils.dataloader.TrajectoryDataset` on `datasets/<scene>/test/`,
  obs 8 × 0.4 s, pred 12 × 0.4 s, skip 1, sequences with ≥ 2 pedestrians — the same
  trajectories `trainval.py --test` scores: eth 181, hotel 1053, univ 24 334, zara1 2253,
  zara2 5833. Leave-one-out checkpoints (the release's convention).
- **Masks.** Per trajectory and rate r ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5}: k = round(8r) of indices 0..6
  dropped without replacement, `np.random.default_rng([seed, scene_idx, round(100 r)])`,
  seed 0. The same mask feeds every arm. Step 7 (t = 2.8 s) is always observed.
- **HOLD**: last observed position repeated. **CV**: velocity from the last two observed
  points and their real stamps.
- **CTRV-repo**: `HumanXZPredictor._predict_future_xz` called unbound on the observed
  `(x, y, t)` tuples, `min_history_for_prediction=3`, `prediction_steps=12`,
  `prediction_secs=4.8`, helpers `_ema_1d`/`_robust_trimmed_mean` from the class. Empty
  output → scored as HOLD, share reported.
- **CTRV-fit**: segment speeds |Δp|/Δt, v = mean clipped to 3 m/s; headings of segments
  ≥ 0.08 m, unwrapped; with ≥ 3 such segments, yaw rate = length-weighted LS slope of heading
  vs segment mid-time, clipped ±1.2 rad/s, zeroed if v < 0.25 m/s or by the repo's guard
  (< 3 segment yaw rates, std > 1.8·|mean|, or |mean| < 0.15); heading = the fit at the last
  stamp; else yaw rate 0, heading = summed displacement. Propagated with the repo's arc
  formulas from the true last position, no decay.
- **EIGEN**: the window with dropped steps imputed — **ffill** (hold last observed; a leading
  gap back-filled from the first observed) = the repo's HELD-frame behaviour, primary; or
  **interp** (`np.interp` per coordinate). Whole sequences are fed together (social model).
  Point rules raw0 / medoid / mean / nearcv; bo20 = min over samples.
- **FUSED**: per step (1−α)·CTRV + α·Eigen(nearcv), CTRV empty → Eigen alone (as
  `_fuse_future_predictions`). α = 0.2 (`EIGEN_TRAJECTORY_FUSION_ALPHA`), α = 0.2 · n_obs/8,
  and constant α ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1}. Both CTRVs fused; the tables show CTRV-fit.
- **Metrics**: ADE = mean over the 12 steps of the L2 error, FDE = step 12; scene mean, then
  AVG = mean of the five scene means.

## 5. Caveats

- **Public checkpoints, not the flown model.** ET-STGCNN trained on ETH/UCY at 2.5 Hz; the
  flight stack feeds the adapter smoothed positions at up to 8 Hz with no resampling, so even
  a real deployment of this checkpoint would see a window 3× shorter in time than it was
  trained on. Nothing about the flown model's quality follows from this study.
- **The learned branch did not fly and is still off.** `eigen_enabled` defaults False;
  `dtp_params.yaml` was not edited. The shim makes `eigen_predictor_kwargs(True)` succeed on
  this box, so **turning it on is now one config key away — do not, without a bench run.**
- **Imputation choice decides the "missing" result** (§2 item 3). ffill is what the code does
  today; interp is what a bridge could trivially do. Report both or state one.
- **The α schedule requested is too gentle.** 0.2·n_obs/8 changes nothing measurable; the
  sweep says the right constant falls from 0.8 to 0 across 0–50 % missing under ffill, and
  stays ~0.2–0.5 under interp.
- **Best-of-1 is a selector problem.** Any Table II "Learned only" row must say which sample
  was taken. bo20 is not a deployable number.
- **The flown CTRV is not the CTRV in the table.** See §2 item 4. If the authors want the
  verbatim code in Table II, it is in `results.csv` as `CTRV-repo`, with its fallback share.
- **Static pedestrians** (~26 % of windows trigger CTRV-repo's fallback at 0 %) are where
  HOLD is the right answer and every mover-tuned arm pays; the per-scene fallback share is a
  proxy for how many there are (hotel 50 %, zara2 44 %).
- **Not subsampled**: the full run took 7.8 min; `--max-seqs` exists but was not used.

## 7. Working with the learned branch — enabling it, tuning it, and whether to retrain

**Where the switch is.** There is no ROS parameter for it. `perception_node.py` constructs
`HumanXZPredictor(..., eigen_enabled=False)` (search for `eigen_enabled` near the
`max_missed_secs` kwarg). To turn the branch on you pass
`**eigen_predictor_kwargs(True)` from `detection_pipeline/eigen.py` in that constructor
instead — it returns `eigen_enabled`, `eigen_hyper_params`, `eigen_checkpoint_path`,
`eigen_device='cuda'` and `eigen_infer_rate_hz=10.0`, or `{}` (with a printed warning) if the
package, config or checkpoint is missing, so requesting it is always safe. Default paths are
`detection_pipeline/EigenTrajectory/config/eigentrajectory-stgcnn-eth.json` and
`…/checkpoints/model_best.pth` (a symlink to the eth checkpoint; point it at any scene's).
Then, per frame, `detection.py` runs `_predict_future_xz_eigen` at 10 Hz and
`_fuse_future_predictions` blends it with CTRV as

    fused = (1 − α) · CTRV + α · Eigen,  α = EIGEN_TRAJECTORY_FUSION_ALPHA = 0.2

(`detection.py` line 20; `eigen_fusion_alpha=` in the constructor overrides it; clipped to [0, 1]).
The model sees the last 8 world-frame (x, y) positions of each track (`eigen_obs_len`) and
returns 12 steps (`eigen_pred_len`); the shim's `forward()` returns 20 samples and the adapter
takes index 0, which the shim defines as the sample nearest the constant-velocity continuation
(raw index 0 is worse than CV — §2).

**Three things to know before flipping it in the stack, none of which the offline study can
settle:**

1. **Time base.** ETH/UCY is 2.5 Hz (0.4 s per step, 3.2 s of observation, 4.8 s of forecast).
   The adapter feeds whatever 8 positions the track has, at perception's ~8 Hz, i.e. 1 s of
   history, and the 12 output steps are then *interpreted* on the 0.4 s grid by
   `_eigen_prediction_to_tuples`. Either resample the history to 0.4 s (drop 7 of 8 frames, and
   wait 3.2 s after lock before the branch has an input) or accept a model run 3× off its
   training cadence. Decide this first; it changes what "retrain" even means.
2. **Frame.** The public model was trained on world-frame trajectories from a fixed overhead
   camera. Ours are world-frame too (`lidar_init`), so no per-frame normalisation is missing, but
   the observer is a moving robot 3–4 m behind the person, so the *distribution* of tracks (a
   single pedestrian walking away from the sensor, no crowd) is nothing like ETH/UCY's.
3. **Cost.** The adapter measured 0.02 s per call on the NX GPU at 10 Hz, on top of YOLO on the
   same GPU. Risk R-F11 (CPU) was the original reason it shipped off; check `perception_node`'s
   per-frame wall time with it on before the rate matters.

**Do you have to tune the weights?** Not to *run* it, and not for the paper's Table II, which
is defined on ETH/UCY. For it to *help in flight*, almost certainly yes, and the study says
where the value would have to come from:

- On complete histories, best-of-1 Eigen (0.54 m ADE) is no better than constant velocity
  (0.52), and CTRV-fit is 0.62. The learned branch's only offline advantage is multimodality
  (best-of-20, 0.23), which a single-goal follower cannot consume directly — but the M6
  reappearance belief *can*: replacing the oracle's 32 CTRV rollouts with the 20 Eigen samples
  is the one place the multimodality has a consumer. That is a design change to
  `loss_oracle.hpp` (rollouts come from outside instead of a CTRV sampler), not a weight change.
- Fine-tuning is feasible with data this stack already records: every bag's `/dtp/targets`
  carries the locked person's world position at 8–10 Hz. Recipe: extract (t, x, y) per track
  from the Stage A and M6.3 bags, resample to 0.4 s, write the ETH/UCY text format
  (`frame_id  ped_id  x  y`, one file per recording, under `EigenTrajectory/datasets/<name>/{train,val,test}/`),
  add a config JSON for the new scene (copy an existing one; `dataset` = the folder name), run
  the public `trainval.py --cfg <json> --tag <tag> --gpu_id 0` (the STGCNN baseline trains in
  well under an hour per scene on a desktop GPU; the NX will do it overnight), then score with
  `m66_predictor_study.py --scenes <name>` against the public checkpoint on the same held-out
  files. Keep a held-out **recording**, not held-out windows from the same walk, or the number
  is meaningless. A few hundred distinct walks is the floor for this to beat CTRV; today's bags
  hold perhaps thirty.
- What is *not* worth tuning: `EIGEN_TRAJECTORY_FUSION_ALPHA`. The sweep (§1) moves ADE by a
  few centimetres for any α between 0 and 0.5 when the imputation is interpolation; the lever
  is the imputation (never forward-fill into the model) and the sample selector, both in the
  adapter/shim, not in the weights.

**Recommendation for the flights in the plan:** leave `eigen_enabled=False` for M5, M6.3 and
M6.7. Report the learned branch in the paper as an evaluated offline option with the public
weights (Table II), state that it did not fly, and, if there is time after D2, try the Eigen
samples as the oracle's rollout source on the M6.3 bags in replay before anything flies.

## 8. The AGX deploy's assets — checked 2026-09-14, nothing to adopt

The "fetch from the AGX" assets arrived as `drone-dtp/weights/EigenTrajectory.tar.xz` (155 MB,
**untracked and not gitignored** — do not `git add -A` it). It is the AGX's whole
`EigenTrajectory/` folder, files dated 2026-04-15 … 05-01: the public repo's code, **all ten
baselines' pretrained checkpoints and configs** (what the release's
`download_pretrained_models.sh` fetches), and their own `eigen.py`. No datasets, no training
logs, no checkpoint of their own.

**The weights are the public ones.** All five `EigenTrajectory-STGCNN/<scene>/model_best.pth`
are sha256-identical to the files §1 was run on, and the five `eigentrajectory-stgcnn-*.json`
are byte-identical. The AGX's `checkpoints/model_best.pth` — the file the adapter loads by
default — is the **hotel** leave-one-out checkpoint (ours symlinks eth). The other nine
baselines were not hash-checked (not downloaded here, and the adapter wires only STGCNN).

**Only the wrapper differs**, and it is worse. Besides a root `__init__.py` and a `.gitignore`
newline, the differing files are `EigenTrajectory/model.py` (an inference-only rewrite of the
public model: builds its baseline itself, no loss path) and `eigen.py`, whose
`EigenTrajectoryInference`:
- **turns the static/moving split off** (`# DISABLE MASKING FOR DEBUG`), so every track goes
  through the moving-pedestrian descriptor and anchors;
- hard-codes `k = 6`, obs 8, pred 12 and the STGCNN shape — the same shape as the checkpoint, so
  it loads strictly;
- returns the samples in raw order, and the adapter takes index 0 (§2 item 2's `raw0`);
- was demonstrated (a commented-out webcam loop) on **normalised pixel coordinates**, not metres.
